## Modernist reference

[V&A, What was Modernism?](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/what-was-modernism). Selected reference: **Drawing**, Edward McKnight Kauffer, 1934, England; museum no. E.3770-2004. Caption credits Victoria and Albert Museum, London. Identification and visual inspection are based on the learner's second modernism screenshot, not the earlier header artwork.

Observed composition: a black human silhouette assembled from rectangular limbs, circular joints, block-like torso forms and an oval head. The extended limbs create diagonal movement across a pale, slanted field; a spiral sits beside one hand. Repeated simple parts create a recognizable whole with strong contrast. The earlier red-and-dark-bar header image was also inspected but is not the selected cited object.

Proposed translation: use a repeatable system of rectangular content regions, consistent spacing and high-contrast hierarchy; preserve one clear path from headline to action. This translates the drawing's economical, repeated parts without copying its figure. Intended effect: make the next step feel easy to locate. Risk: excessive structure may feel impersonal. This drawing alone does not establish claims about modernist production methods or audience response.

## Postmodernist reference

[V&A, What is Postmodernism?](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/what-is-postmodernism). Selected reference: **Cabinet de Curiosité**, cabinet, Shiro Kuramata, 1988, Japan. Caption credits Victoria and Albert Museum, London. Identification and visual inspection are based on the learner-supplied cabinet screenshot. This replaces the lamp as the selected reference; its caption is no longer needed.

Observed composition: a tall transparent cabinet with rectangular inner and outer frames. Green, pink/red and blue segments interrupt the edges, while clear portions make parts of the structure appear to disappear. The inner frame overlaps the outer one, so a conventional storage form becomes an ambiguous display of space and structure. This interpretation is based on the visible image, not a claim to have read the whole article.

Translation: a displaced, open picture frame with differently colored edges suggests overlapping structures; editorial serif lettering and utility labels mix familiar visual conventions. The product remains solid and legible within the playful frame. Intended effect: an expressive but relaxed invitation for young creatives. Risk: the layered frame could compete with the direct action. This is a proposed effect, not audience research.
