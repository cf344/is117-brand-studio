## Research

Website 1: UNIQLO

Source: https://www.uniqlo.com/us/en/products/E424873-000/00

Date researched: November 9, 2026

Observation: UNIQLO's Crew Neck T-Shirt page provides detailed information about the product, including sizes, materials, customer reviews, and styling examples. It also shows the T-shirt on different models so customers can get an idea of how it looks when worn.

What it suggests: The website seems to focus on customers who want simple everyday clothing and enough information to make a decision. The styling examples also help customers imagine how they could wear the T-shirt.

How it helps Off Hours: I like the idea of showing different ways to style one basic white T-shirt. Since Off Hours is about making outfit choices easier, I could include outfit examples and keep the product information simple and organized.

Website 2: H&M

Source: https://www2.hm.com/en_us/productpage.1262644004.html

Date researched: November 9, 2026

Observation: H&M's Regular Fit T-Shirt page shows product photographs, the price, and options to add the item to a bag or check store availability. It also provides information about the fit, fabric, and style. The page displays sale pricing when applicable.

What it suggests: The website appears to help customers compare clothing based on appearance, price, and product details. Showing this information together can make shopping more convenient.

How it helps Off Hours: I could use clear product photographs and an easy-to-find size selector. I also want the website to make it simple for customers to understand what they are choosing without having to search through too much information.

Website 3: Zara

Source: https://www.zara.com/us/en/basic-cotton-t-shirt-p03253320.html

Date researched: November 9, 2026

Observation: Zara's Basic Cotton T-Shirt page uses a simple product presentation with a large product image, product name, price, and purchasing option. Additional information, such as measurements, composition, care, and shipping, is available in separate sections.

What it suggests: The page puts attention on the clothing itself while keeping the main shopping information easy to find. This approach may appeal to customers who prefer a more fashion-focused presentation.

How it helps Off Hours: I could use a clean design with large pictures of the white T-shirt. This would help the product stand out while allowing customers to focus on outfit ideas instead of unnecessary decorations.

## 1. Comparison

After researching the three websites, I noticed that they all provide important product information, but they emphasize different things.

W| Website | Main Focus | What I Can Learn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UNIQLO | Everyday basics and styling | Show outfit ideas |
| H&M | Shopping convenience | Make information easy to find |
| Zara | Simple product presentation | Use clean visuals |

My conclusion: I think Off Hours could combine ideas from all three websites. I would use UNIQLO's styling inspiration, H&M's clear shopping information, and Zara's simple product presentation. This would help create a website that feels modern and creative without being complicated.

## 2. Comparing 2 brands

Position A: Effortless Everyday Style

This position focuses on making everyday outfits simple and easy to put together. The white T-shirt would be presented as a basic clothing item that can be styled in different ways for school, casual outings, or spending time with friends.

The website would have a clean layout, neutral colors, and outfit examples. The main message would be that customers do not need to spend a lot of time choosing what to wear.

Position B: Trendy and Creative Fashion

This position focuses more on self-expression and creating unique outfits. The white T-shirt would be presented as a starting point for fashionable looks.

The website could use bold colors, creative typography, and more expressive photographs. It would focus on helping customers show their personalities through clothing.

My Choice

I chose Position A: Effortless Everyday Style because it matches the main idea behind Off Hours.

I want the brand to help young people find casual outfits that look good without making getting dressed complicated. I also think I can still include creative outfit ideas while keeping the website simple and easy to use.

## 3. Two Customer Persona Hypotheses

Persona 1: Young Creative

Name: Jordan
Age: 22
Background: A young adult who enjoys going out with friends and trying different casual styles.

Goal: Jordan wants outfits that look stylish and express their personality without spending too much time planning them.

Concern: Jordan might find basic clothing boring or struggle to decide how to style it.

What the website should provide: Different outfit ideas showing how one white T-shirt can be styled for different casual occasions.

Assumption: I assume that young creative people would find outfit inspiration helpful. This has not been confirmed through customer interviews.

Persona 2: College Student

Name: Alex
Age: 19
Background: A college student who attends classes and spends time on campus.

Goal: Alex wants simple everyday outfits that are easy to choose before going to class.

Concern: Alex may care about price, sizing, comfort, and whether the clothing is practical for everyday use.

What the website should provide: Clear product information, simple styling examples, and easy navigation.

Assumption: I assume that college students value convenience and clear information when shopping for everyday clothes. More research would be needed to confirm this.

## 4. Choosing My Target Audience

Primary Audience: Young creative people who want effortless casual outfits.

Secondary Audience: College students who want simple everyday clothing.

I chose young creatives as my primary audience because I want Off Hours to be a brand that helps people look stylish without spending too much time choosing outfits.

College students are my secondary audience because they may have similar needs, especially when looking for quick and simple outfits for class.

Both audiences could benefit from a website that provides outfit inspiration and makes clothing choices easier.

## 5. Visual Style and Brand Character

Visual Style

For Off Hours, I chose a clean, modernist-inspired design with simple typography, neutral colors, organized sections, and clear product photographs.

I want the website to look modern and creative but still feel easy to navigate. I would also include outfit examples to show how the same white T-shirt can work with different styles.

Brand Character

The main brand archetype is the Everyperson.

This means the brand should feel friendly, relatable, casual, and welcoming. I do not want Off Hours to feel exclusive or overly luxurious.

The brand's message is:

"Less time choosing. More time doing your thing."

This represents the idea that getting dressed should be simple so people can spend more time enjoying their day.

## 6. Honest Persuasion

I want Off Hours to encourage customers to explore the product without misleading them.

Persuasion Method 1: Liking

I would use relatable outfit photographs and friendly language to help customers connect with the brand. Showing casual outfits for everyday activities could make the website feel more approachable.

Persuasion Method 2: Reciprocity

I would provide free outfit inspiration and styling suggestions that visitors can use even if they do not purchase anything. This gives customers something useful and may encourage them to explore the brand.

I would avoid fake customer reviews, misleading discounts, or unsupported claims about the T-shirt's quality and comfort.

## 7. Assumptions and Limitations

Some of my research includes assumptions that have not been confirmed.

I assume young creatives would appreciate outfit inspiration.

I assume college students want simple and convenient shopping experiences.

I assume a clean website layout would make choosing outfits easier.

I have not interviewed real customers to confirm these assumptions.

I have not verified the actual fabric, comfort, durability, or manufacturing details of the Off Hours T-shirt.

The three website comparisons are based on product pages, not interviews with the companies' customers. My conclusions about their target audiences are interpretations of their website designs.

## 8. Final Research Conclusion

After comparing UNIQLO, H&M, and Zara, I learned that a clothing website should do more than just show products. It should also help customers understand what they are buying and imagine how they could wear it.

For Off Hours, I want to create a website that combines simple design, useful product information, and creative outfit inspiration.

My main goal is to help young people choose casual outfits easily while still feeling confident in their personal style.
